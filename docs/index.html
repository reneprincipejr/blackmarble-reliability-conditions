Satellite evidence for disaster recovery

Watching disaster recovery from space

Interactive figures from a research workflow testing when nighttime satellite lights can be trusted as evidence of electricity disruption and recovery after major storms.

Problem Clouds and data gaps can make daily nighttime lights misleading.
Approach Screen the satellite record before interpreting disaster signals.
Use case Support recovery monitoring where ground information is delayed or fragmented.

The story in one sentence

Nighttime lights can reveal disruption and recovery after disasters, but only when the satellite actually observed enough valid pixels to make the signal trustworthy.

Key takeaway: A smooth satellite time series is not automatically reliable. In tropical disaster settings, observability must be checked before nighttime lights are interpreted as evidence of power recovery.

How to read the evidence

The figures below are interactive. You can zoom, pan, hover, and open each figure in a larger view. Read them as evidence layers, not as a single definitive answer.

Direct light signal

DNB-BRDF radiance is the directly observed nightlight signal. A drop may indicate outage, damage, evacuation, or reduced activity.

Gap-filled signal

Gap-filled radiance can look continuous because it may use previous observations. It is useful context, but not always fresh evidence.

Spatial completeness

Spatial completeness measures how much of the area was validly observed. Low completeness means the signal should be treated cautiously.

What stakeholders can take from this

For disaster agencies, infrastructure planners, energy analysts, and resilience researchers, this work provides both a caution and an opportunity.

The caution is that nighttime lights should not be interpreted blindly. Cloud cover, data gaps, and low-light conditions can produce misleading signals.

The opportunity is that, with reliability screening, nighttime lights can become an independent layer of evidence for identifying where disruption and recovery may be visible from space.
